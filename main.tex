\section{Physical Modelling}
\subsection{Kinematics}

The CoreXY mechanism couples the motion of both motors to the Cartesian
motion of the print head. The linear displacement
of each belt is related to the corresponding motor rotation through the
motor pulley radius $r_m$.

Using the chosen sign convention, the belt displacements satisfy
\begin{equation}
r_m\theta_l = x+y,
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
r_m\theta_r = x-y.
\end{equation}

Solving for the Cartesian coordinates gives
\begin{equation}
x = \frac{r_m}{2}\left(\theta_l+\theta_r\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
y = \frac{r_m}{2}\left(\theta_l-\theta_r\right).
\end{equation}

Differentiating with respect to time yields the print-head velocities:
\begin{equation}
\dot{x} = \frac{r_m}{2}
\left(\dot{\theta}_l+\dot{\theta}_r\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\dot{y} = \frac{r_m}{2}
\left(\dot{\theta}_l-\dot{\theta}_r\right).
\end{equation}
\subsection{Dynamics}
\paragraph{Electrical dynamics.}
Since the left and right motor circuits are identical, they can be
described simultaneously by introducing the index $j\in\{l,r\}$.
Applying Kirchhoff's voltage law gives
\begin{equation}
u_j = L_m\dot{i}_j + R_m i_j + u_{M,j},
\qquad j\in\{l,r\},
\end{equation}
where $u_{M,j}$ denotes the voltage across the electromechanical
transducer.\\
Rearranging,
\begin{equation}
\dot{i}_j=
\frac{1}{L_m}
\left(
u_j-R_m i_j-u_{M,j}
\right),
\qquad j\in\{l,r\}.
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Electromechanical transducer.}
The DC motor couples the electrical and rotational mechanical domains
through the motor constant $K_m$. The voltage across the transducer is
proportional to the angular velocity, while the generated motor torque is
proportional to the armature current:
\begin{equation}
u_{M,j}=K_m\dot{\theta}_j,
\qquad j\in\{l,r\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\tau_j=K_m i_j,
\qquad j\in\{l,r\}.
\end{equation}
\paragraph{Mechanical dynamics.}
The mechanical model includes the forces transmitted by the two belts,
the friction of the linear bearings, and the rolling friction generated
by the rotational bearings.

The linear bearings are modelled through viscous friction. Since the
print head is constrained by two linear guides along each Cartesian
direction, the friction contribution is doubled. Therefore,
\begin{equation}
F_{l,x}=2\mu_{l,x}\dot{x},
\qquad
F_{l,y}=2\mu_{l,y}\dot{y}.
\end{equation}
The rolling friction requires a more detailed treatment, since not all rotational bearings experience the same relative belt velocity. For each belt, the bearings fixed to the frame experience the velocity of the corresponding belt,
\[
r_m\dot{\theta}_j, \qquad j\in\{l,r\}.
\]

The remaining bearings move together with the gantry in the $y$-direction. Their relative velocities are therefore
\begin{equation}
v_{r,l}^{m}=r_m\dot{\theta}_l-\dot{y},
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
v_{r,r}^{m}=r_m\dot{\theta}_r+\dot{y}.
\end{equation}

Using the kinematic relations derived in Section 1.1,
\[
r_m\dot{\theta}_l=\dot{x}+\dot{y},
\qquad
r_m\dot{\theta}_r=\dot{x}-\dot{y},
\]
the moving bearings experience a relative velocity equal to $\dot{x}$. Therefore, they contribute only to the friction force along the $x$-direction.

Along the $x$-direction, all rolling bearings contribute, giving
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
F_{r,x} ={}&
-2\mu_r r_m\dot{\theta}_l
-2\mu_r\left(r_m\dot{\theta}_l-\dot{y}\right)\\
&-2\mu_r r_m\dot{\theta}_r
-2\mu_r\left(r_m\dot{\theta}_r+\dot{y}\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

Along the $y$-direction, only the fixed bearings contribute. Since the left and right belts experience the $y$-motion with opposite signs, the rolling-friction contribution is
\begin{equation}
F_{r,y}
=
-2\mu_r r_m\dot{\theta}_l
+2\mu_r r_m\dot{\theta}_r.
\end{equation}

Applying Newton's second law to the print head gives
\begin{equation}
m\ddot{x}
=
F_l+F_r
-2\mu_{l,x}\dot{x}
+F_{r,x},
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
m\ddot{y}
=
F_l-F_r
-2\mu_{l,y}\dot{y}
+F_{r,y},
\end{equation}
where the forces transmitted by the belts are related to the motor torques through the rotor dynamics,
\[
J_m\ddot{\theta}_j=\tau_j-r_mF_j,
\qquad j\in\{l,r\},
\]
so that
\[
F_j=
\frac{\tau_j-J_m\ddot{\theta}_j}{r_m},
\qquad j\in\{l,r\}.
\]
\paragraph{Alternative Euler--Lagrange derivation.}
The mechanical dynamics can alternatively be derived using the
Euler--Lagrange equations with $x$ and $y$ as generalized coordinates.

Using the kinematic relations
\[
\theta_l=\frac{x+y}{r_m},
\qquad
\theta_r=\frac{x-y}{r_m},
\]
the total kinetic energy is
\begin{equation}
T=
\frac{1}{2}m\left(\dot{x}^2+\dot{y}^2\right)
+
\frac{1}{2}J_m\dot{\theta}_l^2
+
\frac{1}{2}J_m\dot{\theta}_r^2.
\end{equation}

The viscous losses are described through the Rayleigh dissipation function
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}
=
\mu_{l,x}\dot{x}^2
+
\mu_{l,y}\dot{y}^2
+
4\mu_r\dot{x}^2
+
2\mu_r\dot{y}^2.
\end{equation}

Applying
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{dt}
\left(
\frac{\partial L}{\partial \dot{q}_i}
\right)
-
\frac{\partial L}{\partial q_i}
+
\frac{\partial \mathcal{D}}{\partial \dot{q}_i}
=
Q_i,
\end{equation}
with $L=T$ and
\[
Q_x=\frac{\tau_l+\tau_r}{r_m},
\qquad
Q_y=\frac{\tau_l-\tau_r}{r_m},
\]
yields
\begin{equation}
\left(
m+\frac{2J_m}{r_m^2}
\right)\ddot{x}
+
\left(
2\mu_{l,x}+8\mu_r
\right)\dot{x}
=
\frac{\tau_l+\tau_r}{r_m},
\end{equation}

\begin{equation}
\left(
m+\frac{2J_m}{r_m^2}
\right)\ddot{y}
+
\left(
2\mu_{l,y}+4\mu_r
\right)\dot{y}
=
\frac{\tau_l-\tau_r}{r_m}.
\end{equation}

These equations coincide with the mechanical model obtained above through the force-balance approach.

\paragraph{Complete system dynamics.}
By substituting the transducer relations into the motor equations,
using the belt-force relation, and finally introducing the kinematic relations derived in Section 1.1,
the complete set of differential equations describing the coupled dynamics of the two motors and the print head is obtained as
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{\theta}_l &= \omega_l,\\
\dot{\theta}_r &= \omega_r,\\
\dot{\omega}_l &=
\frac{K_m}{J_{\mathrm{eq}}}i_l
-
\frac{r_m^2}{2J_{\mathrm{eq}}}
\left[
(c_x+c_y)\omega_l
+
(c_x-c_y)\omega_r
\right],\\
\dot{\omega}_r &=
\frac{K_m}{J_{\mathrm{eq}}}i_r
-
\frac{r_m^2}{2J_{\mathrm{eq}}}
\left[
(c_x-c_y)\omega_l
+
(c_x+c_y)\omega_r
\right],\\
\dot{i}_l &=
\frac{1}{L_m}
\left(
u_l-R_m i_l-K_m\omega_l
\right),\\
\dot{i}_r &=
\frac{1}{L_m}
\left(
u_r-R_m i_r-K_m\omega_r
\right).
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}

where, for compactness,
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{eq}} = J_m+\frac{mr_m^2}{2},
\qquad
c_x=\mu_{l,x}+4\mu_r,
\qquad
c_y=\mu_{l,y}+2\mu_r.
\end{equation}
\subsection{Decoupling of the $x$ and $y$ Dynamics}

The coupled motor dynamics can be decoupled by exploiting the structure of
the CoreXY kinematics. From Section 1.1,
\[
\dot{x}=\frac{r_m}{2}\left(\omega_l+\omega_r\right),
\qquad
\dot{y}=\frac{r_m}{2}\left(\omega_l-\omega_r\right).
\]

Therefore,
\[
\omega_l+\omega_r=\frac{2\dot{x}}{r_m},
\qquad
\omega_l-\omega_r=\frac{2\dot{y}}{r_m}.
\]

Starting from the mechanical equations derived in Section 1.2, summing the
left and right motor equations gives
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{eq}}\left(\dot{\omega}_l+\dot{\omega}_r\right)
=
K_m(i_l+i_r)
-r_m^2c_x(\omega_l+\omega_r),
\end{equation}
while subtracting them gives
\begin{equation}
J_{\mathrm{eq}}\left(\dot{\omega}_l-\dot{\omega}_r\right)
=
K_m(i_l-i_r)
-r_m^2c_y(\omega_l-\omega_r).
\end{equation}

Using the kinematic relations above, these equations become
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{eq}}\ddot{x}
=
-2c_x\dot{x}
+
\frac{K_m}{r_m}(i_l+i_r),
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{eq}}\ddot{y}
=
-2c_y\dot{y}
+
\frac{K_m}{r_m}(i_l-i_r),
\end{equation}
where
\begin{equation}
M_{\mathrm{eq}}
=
m+\frac{2J_m}{r_m^2}.
\end{equation}

The same sum-and-difference transformation can be applied to the electrical
equations. Summing them gives
\begin{equation}
L_m(\dot{i}_l+\dot{i}_r)
=
(u_l+u_r)
-R_m(i_l+i_r)
-\frac{2K_m}{r_m}\dot{x},
\end{equation}
while subtracting them gives
\begin{equation}
L_m(\dot{i}_l-\dot{i}_r)
=
(u_l-u_r)
-R_m(i_l-i_r)
-\frac{2K_m}{r_m}\dot{y}.
\end{equation}

This naturally suggests introducing the transformed variables
\begin{equation}
i_x=i_l+i_r,
\qquad
i_y=i_l-i_r,
\end{equation}
and
\begin{equation}
u_x=u_l+u_r,
\qquad
u_y=u_l-u_r.
\end{equation}

With these definitions, the original coupled system separates into two
independent subsystems.

For the $x$-direction,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{x} &= v_x,\\
\dot{v}_x &=
-\frac{2c_x}{M_{\mathrm{eq}}}v_x
+
\frac{K_m}{r_mM_{\mathrm{eq}}}i_x,\\
\dot{i}_x &=
-\frac{2K_m}{L_mr_m}v_x
-\frac{R_m}{L_m}i_x
+\frac{1}{L_m}u_x,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}


For the $y$-direction,
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\dot{y} &= v_y,\\
\dot{v}_y &=
-\frac{2c_y}{M_{\mathrm{eq}}}v_y
+
\frac{K_m}{r_mM_{\mathrm{eq}}}i_y,\\
\dot{i}_y &=
-\frac{2K_m}{L_mr_m}v_y
-\frac{R_m}{L_m}i_y
+\frac{1}{L_m}u_y,
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
where,
\begin{equation}
c_x=\mu_{l,x}+4\mu_r,
\qquad
c_y=\mu_{l,y}+2\mu_r.
\end{equation}

Hence, the sum of the motor variables governs the $x$-motion, while their
difference governs the $y$-motion, resulting in two fully decoupled
third-order systems.
\subsection{Drawing Shapes}

To illustrate the motion capabilities of the CoreXY model, a complete
printing sequence is considered. The tool head starts from the home
position $(0,0)$, moves to the initial point of the desired trajectory,
waits for a short settling interval, prints the desired shape, waits
again after completing the path, and finally returns to the home
position.

A binary variable
\[
p(t)\in\{0,1\}
\]
is introduced to distinguish between printing and non-printing motion.
In particular, $p(t)=1$ only during material deposition, whereas
$p(t)=0$ during positioning, waiting, and return-to-home motion.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Input generation}

For a prescribed trajectory $(x_d(t),y_d(t))$, the decoupled dynamics
derived in Section~1.3 give
\[
M_{\mathrm{eq}}\ddot{x}+B_x\dot{x}
=
\frac{K_m}{r_m}i_x,
\qquad
M_{\mathrm{eq}}\ddot{y}+B_y\dot{y}
=
\frac{K_m}{r_m}i_y,
\]
where
\[
M_{\mathrm{eq}}=m+\frac{2J_m}{r_m^2},
\qquad
B_x=2\mu_{l,x}+8\mu_r,
\qquad
B_y=2\mu_{l,y}+4\mu_r.
\]

The required transformed currents are therefore obtained through inverse
dynamics:
\[
i_x=
\frac{r_m}{K_m}
\left(M_{\mathrm{eq}}\ddot{x}_d+B_x\dot{x}_d\right),
\qquad
i_y=
\frac{r_m}{K_m}
\left(M_{\mathrm{eq}}\ddot{y}_d+B_y\dot{y}_d\right).
\]

Their derivatives are then used in the electrical equations:
\[
u_x=L_m\dot{i}_x+R_mi_x+\frac{2K_m}{r_m}\dot{x}_d,
\qquad
u_y=L_m\dot{i}_y+R_mi_y+\frac{2K_m}{r_m}\dot{y}_d.
\]

Finally, the physical motor voltages are
\[
u_l=\frac{u_x+u_y}{2},
\qquad
u_r=\frac{u_x-u_y}{2}.
\]

Hence, the input-generation procedure can be summarised as
\[
(x_d,y_d)
\rightarrow
(i_x,i_y)
\rightarrow
(u_x,u_y)
\rightarrow
(u_l,u_r).
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Positioning and printing sequence}

Before printing, the head moves from $(0,0)$ to the starting point
$(x_s,y_s)$ at constant speed $v_{\mathrm{pos}}$. The corresponding
distance and travelling time are
\[
d_{\mathrm{out}}=\sqrt{x_s^2+y_s^2},
\qquad
T_{\mathrm{move}}=\frac{d_{\mathrm{out}}}{v_{\mathrm{pos}}},
\]
with Cartesian velocity components
\[
v_x=v_{\mathrm{pos}}\frac{x_s}{d_{\mathrm{out}}},
\qquad
v_y=v_{\mathrm{pos}}\frac{y_s}{d_{\mathrm{out}}}.
\]

The short acceleration and deceleration transients associated with the
positioning motion are neglected. After reaching the starting point,
the system waits for a settling interval \(T_{\mathrm{wait}}\), prints one complete closed
trajectory, waits again, and returns to the home position at the same
positioning speed.


\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Elliptical trajectory}

The first path is an ellipse centred in the printing area,
\[
x_c=y_c=\frac{L_{\mathrm{bed}}}{2}=0.20~\mathrm{m},
\]
and parameterised as
\[
x_d(\tau)=x_c+a\cos(\omega\tau),
\qquad
y_d(\tau)=y_c+b\sin(\omega\tau),
\]
with
\[
a=0.15~\mathrm{m},
\qquad
b=0.10~\mathrm{m},
\qquad
\omega=\frac{2\pi}{10}~\mathrm{rad/s}.
\]

The trajectory starts at
\[
(x_s,y_s)=(0.35,0.20)~\mathrm{m}
\]
and is completed when $\omega\tau=2\pi$, hence
\[
T_{\mathrm{ellipse}}=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}=10~\mathrm{s}.
\]

The required first, second and third derivatives are obtained analytically
and substituted into the inverse-dynamics expressions above.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Four-leaf clover trajectory}

As a second closed path, a clover-shaped trajectory was considered using
the same printer model, physical parameters and printing sequence.
The clover trajectory is based on the four-petal rose curve
$r=a\sin(2\theta)$ \cite{rose_curve}, converted to Cartesian
coordinates through $x=r\cos\theta$ and $y=r\sin\theta$.
Its Cartesian parameterisation is
\[
x_d(\tau)
=
x_c+a\sin(2\omega\tau)\cos(\omega\tau),
\]
\[
y_d(\tau)
=
y_c+a\sin(2\omega\tau)\sin(\omega\tau),
\]
with
\[
x_c=y_c=0.20~\mathrm{m},
\qquad
a=0.15~\mathrm{m}.
\]

The trajectory starts at the centre of the printing area,
\[
(x_s,y_s)=(0.20,0.20)~\mathrm{m},
\]
and is completed when $\omega\tau=2\pi$, hence
\[
T_{\mathrm{clover}}=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}=10~\mathrm{s}.
\]

As for the ellipse, the required derivatives are obtained analytically
and used in the same inverse-dynamics formulation.

\subsection{Nozzle Model}
The nozzle heating system is modelled by coupling the electrical circuit
to a thermal model. The input of the system is the controllable
voltage $u_n$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Electrical dynamics}

Let $V_c$ denote the voltage across the resistor-capacitor branch and
$i_L$ the current through the inductor. The resulting relations are
\[
i_C=C_n\dot V_c,
\qquad
V_L=L_n\dot i_L.
\]

Applying Kirchhoff's voltage law gives
\[
u_n=V_c+V_L,
\]
and therefore
\[
\dot i_L
=
-\frac{V_c}{L_n}
+
\frac{u_n}{L_n}.
\]

Using Kirchhoff's current law,
\[
i_L=i_R+i_C,
\]
with
\[
i_R=\frac{V_c}{R_n},
\]
the capacitor dynamics become
\[
\dot V_c
=
\frac{i_L}{C_n}
-
\frac{V_c}{R_nC_n}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Thermal dynamics}

The electrical power dissipated by the resistor is converted into heat by the Joule Effect:
\[
P_n
=
R_ni_R^2
=
\frac{V_c^2}{R_n}.
\]

The nozzle is modelled as a thermal capacity $C_{t,n}$ exchanging
heat with the environment through the thermal resistance $R_{t,n}$. The
thermal balance is therefore
\[
C_{t,n}\dot T_n
=
\frac{V_c^2}{R_n}
-
\frac{T_n-T_e}{R_{t,n}},
\]
which gives
\[
\dot T_n
=
\frac{V_c^2}{R_nC_{t,n}}
-
\frac{T_n-T_e}{C_{t,n}R_{t,n}}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{State-space representation}

Defining the nozzle state as
\[
x_n=
\begin{bmatrix}
T_n & V_c & i_L
\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\]
the system can be written in the required form
\[
\dot{x}_n=f_n(x_n,u_n),
\]
with governing differential equations
\[
\dot T_n
=
\frac{V_c^2}{R_nC_{t,n}}
-
\frac{T_n-T_e}{C_{t,n}R_{t,n}},
\]
\[
\dot V_c
=
\frac{i_L}{C_n}
-
\frac{V_c}{R_nC_n},
\]
\[
\dot i_L
=
-\frac{V_c}{L_n}
+
\frac{u_n}{L_n}.
\]
\subsection{Filament Cooling}

The printed filament is modelled as a 1-D distributed thermal system with a square
cross section of side length $w$. The spatial coordinate $x$ is defined along the deposited
filament. During printing, the length of the computational domain increases according to

\[
L(t)=v_{\mathrm{print}}t,
\qquad 0\leq t\leq t_{\mathrm{print}},
\]

whereas after the printing process is completed,

\[
L(t)=L_{\mathrm{tot}},
\qquad t\geq t_{\mathrm{print}}.
\]

The temperature along the filament is denoted by $T(x,t)$.

\smallskip
\noindent\textbf{Heat equation}

The thermal energy balance per unit volume is written as

\[
\rho c_p\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
=
-\nabla\cdot\phi + f,
\]

where $\rho$ is the filament density, $c_p$ its specific heat capacity,
$\phi$ is the conductive heat flux, and $f$ represents distributed heat exchange with the
surroundings.

Using Fourier's law,

\[
\phi=-k_{\mathrm{filament}}\nabla T,
\]

the governing equation becomes

\[
\rho c_p\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
=
k_{\mathrm{filament}}\nabla^2T+f.
\]

Since the model is one-dimensional along the filament coordinate,

\[
\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
=
\frac{k_{\mathrm{filament}}}{\rho c_p}
\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial x^2}
+
\frac{f}{\rho c_p}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Heat exchange with air and heated plate}

The filament has a square cross section,

\[
A=w^2.
\]

Three sides are exposed to the surrounding air at temperature $T_e$, while the lower side is in
contact with the heated plate at temperature $T_b$.

The distributed heat exchange per unit length is therefore modelled as

\[
q'(x,t)
=
3wh_{\mathrm{air}}\left(T_e-T(x,t)\right)
+
wh_{\mathrm{plate}}\left(T_b-T(x,t)\right),
\]

where $h_{\mathrm{air}}$ and $h_{\mathrm{plate}}$ are effective heat-transfer coefficients.

Dividing by the cross-sectional area gives the corresponding volumetric source term,

\[
f(x,t)
=
\frac{1}{A}
\left[
3wh_{\mathrm{air}}\left(T_e-T(x,t)\right)
+
wh_{\mathrm{plate}}\left(T_b-T(x,t)\right)
\right].
\]

Hence, the final PDE is

\[
\frac{\partial T}{\partial t}
=
\frac{k_{\mathrm{filament}}}{\rho c_p}
\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial x^2}
+
\frac{
3wh_{\mathrm{air}}(T_e-T)
+
wh_{\mathrm{plate}}(T_b-T)
}{
\rho c_p w^2
}
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Spatial discretisation}

The filament is divided into equally spaced sections of length $\Delta x$. For an interior node
$i$, the second spatial derivative is approximated using a central finite difference,

\[
\frac{\partial^2T}{\partial x^2}
\approx
\frac{T_{i+1}-2T_i+T_{i-1}}{\Delta x^2}.
\]

Therefore, the temperature dynamics at node $i$ are

\[
\frac{dT_i}{dt}
=
\frac{k_{\mathrm{filament}}}{\rho c_p}
\frac{T_{i+1}-2T_i+T_{i-1}}{\Delta x^2}
+
\frac{
3wh_{\mathrm{air}}(T_e-T_i)
+
wh_{\mathrm{plate}}(T_b-T_i)
}{
\rho c_p w^2
}.
\]

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Boundary conditions during printing}

During deposition, the computational domain grows in time. The temperature at the active
printing front is assumed equal to the nozzle temperature,

\[
T(L(t),t)=T_n.
\]

At the initial end of the deposited filament, a zero heat-flux condition is imposed,

\[
\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}(0,t)=0.
\]

As the nozzle moves, new spatial nodes are progressively activated and initialised at temperature
$T_n$.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Boundary conditions after printing}

Once the complete closed path has been deposited, the computational domain has fixed length
$L_{\mathrm{tot}}$. Since the physical path is closed, periodic boundary conditions are applied,

\[
T(0,t)=T(L_{\mathrm{tot}},t),
\]

and

\[
\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}(0,t)
=
\frac{\partial T}{\partial x}(L_{\mathrm{tot}},t).
\]

The simulation is continued until the temperature becomes nearly uniform. This condition is
defined as

\[
\max_i T_i-\min_i T_i\leq \varepsilon,
\]

where $\varepsilon$ is a prescribed temperature tolerance.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{MATLAB Implementation and Simulation}

The discretised thermal model was implemented in MATLAB using an explicit Euler scheme.
During printing, the computational domain grows as new filament is deposited. New nodes are
activated according to the printing speed and are initialised at the nozzle temperature \(T_n\).
The zero heat-flux boundary condition is applied at the initial end of the filament.

Once the complete path has been deposited, the domain is kept fixed and periodic boundary
conditions are applied to represent the closed printed path. The simulation is then continued
until

\[
\max_i T_i-\min_i T_i\leq\varepsilon.
\]

With the selected values, the complete filament is deposited in

\[
t_{\mathrm{print}}
=
\frac{L_{\mathrm{tot}}}{v_{\mathrm{print}}}
=
5\,\mathrm{s}.
\]

The temperature profiles at the end of printing and after cooling are shown in
Figure~\ref{fig:filament_cooling}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\textwidth]{FilamentCooling.png}
    \caption{Temperature distribution along the filament at the end of printing and after cooling.}
    \label{fig:filament_cooling}
\end{figure}

At the end of printing, the temperature is not uniform because the first deposited sections
have already started cooling, while the most recent sections are still close to the nozzle
temperature. After approximately \(21.3\,\mathrm{s}\) from the beginning of the simulation,
the temperature difference along the filament becomes smaller than the selected tolerance $(tol. = 5[K])$.
Consequently, the filament has final nearly uniform temperature.
\section{Finite State Machines}

\subsection{Filament Replacement}

The filament-replacement procedure is triggered either when a new colour is required,
denoted by $C$, or when the current filament spool is finished, denoted by $F$.
In both cases, printing is interrupted and the current print-head position is stored.
The print head is then moved to the wipe-tower location
\[
(x_p,y_p)=(0,0),
\]
where the excess filament is removed. The nozzle heater is subsequently switched off,
the new filament is loaded, and the nozzle is heated again. Before resuming the print,
the print head returns to the previously stored position.

The controller is represented as a Moore-type finite-state machine. Its state set is
\[
Q =
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{Printing},\ \text{Stop \& Save},\ \text{Move to wipe tower},\ \text{Wiping},\\
\text{Heat off},\ \text{Load filament},\ \text{Heat on},\ \text{Moving back}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]
with initial-state set
\[
Q_0=\{\text{Printing}\}.
\]

The input alphabet consists of the discrete events triggering state transitions,
\[
\Sigma =
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
C,\ F,\ \text{position saved},\ \text{wipe location reached},\\
\text{excess filament removed},\ \text{temperature low},\\
\text{filament loaded},\ \text{temperature high},\\
\text{saved position reached}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]

The corresponding output alphabet, representing the action performed in each state, is
\[
\Gamma =
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{Print},\ \text{Stop and save position},\ \text{Move to }(0,0),\\
\text{Print at wipe tower},\ \text{Heater OFF},\ \text{Load filament},\\
\text{Heater ON},\ \text{Return to saved position}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]

The resulting finite-state machine, including the state transitions and the action associated
with each state, is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:filament_fsm}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{FST.png}
    \caption{Finite-state machine for the filament replacement procedure.}
    \label{fig:filament_fsm}
\end{figure}

\subsection{Parked Detection}

The print head is brought to the origin by commanding horizontal and vertical motions until
the corresponding boundaries are detected. A boundary is identified when a motion command
is applied while the measured motor angular velocity is zero.

The input alphabet is
\[
\Sigma=\{H_{+},H_{0},V_{+},V_{0}\},
\]
where $H$ and $V$ denote horizontal and vertical motion commands, respectively, while
$+$ and $0$ indicate positive and zero angular velocity.

The automaton is defined by the state set
\[
Q=
\left\{
\begin{array}{l}
\text{No boundary reached},\ 
\text{Horizontal boundary reached},\\
\text{Vertical boundary reached},\ 
\text{Head correctly parked}
\end{array}
\right\}.
\]

The initial state is
\[
Q_0=\{\text{No boundary reached}\},
\]
while the accepting-state set is
\[
F=\{\text{Head correctly parked}\}.
\]

As long as the measured angular velocity remains positive, no new boundary is detected.
Therefore, $H_{+}$ and $V_{+}$ leave the automaton in the current detection state.
A zero angular velocity instead indicates that the corresponding boundary has been reached.
Once one boundary has been detected, only the remaining motion direction needs to be checked.
The head is considered correctly parked after both boundaries have been detected.

The resulting automaton is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:parked_automaton}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.75\linewidth]{Automata.png}
    \caption{Automaton for the detection of the correctly parked print head.}
    \label{fig:parked_automaton}
\end{figure}
\section{Hybrid System}

\subsection{Filament Replacement}

Firstly, we collect the continuous dynamics derived in Section~1 into a single state vector,

\[
x =
\begin{bmatrix}
x & v_x & i_x &
y & v_y & i_y &
T_n & V_c & i_L
\end{bmatrix}^{\top},
\]

with continuous input vector

\[
u =
\begin{bmatrix}
u_x & u_y & u_n
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}.
\]

The continuous dynamics of the hybrid model are defined as

\[
\dot{x}=f(q,x,u),
\]

where \(q\in Q\) denotes the active discrete mode. The set of discrete modes is inherited from the filament-replacement FSM introduced in Section~2.1:

\[
Q=
\{
q_0,q_1,q_2,q_3,q_4,q_5,q_6,q_7
\},
\]

with

\[
\begin{aligned}
q_0 &: \text{Printing},\\
q_1 &: \text{Stop and save current position},\\
q_2 &: \text{Move to wipe tower},\\
q_3 &: \text{Wiping},\\
q_4 &: \text{Nozzle heat off},\\
q_5 &: \text{Load new filament},\\
q_6 &: \text{Nozzle heat on},\\
q_7 &: \text{Moving back to the saved position}.
\end{aligned}
\]

The active mode determines the continuous inputs and whether the nozzle deposits material.
The binary deposition signal introduced in Section~1.4 is defined as

\[
p=p(q)\in\{0,1\},
\]

with \(p=1\) when material is deposited and \(p=0\) otherwise.

The continuous inputs and deposition signal associated with each mode are defined as

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{Mode} & u & p \\ \hline

q_0:\ \text{Printing}
&
\begin{bmatrix}
u_x^{\mathrm{print}} &
u_y^{\mathrm{print}} &
u_n
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
& 1
\\[3mm]

q_1:\ \text{Stop \& Save}
&
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & u_n
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
& 0
\\[3mm]

q_2:\ \text{Move to wipe tower}
&
\begin{bmatrix}
u_x^{\mathrm{parking}} &
u_y^{\mathrm{parking}} &
u_n
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
& 0
\\[3mm]

q_3:\ \text{Wiping}
&
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & u_n
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
& 1
\\[3mm]

q_4:\ \text{Nozzle heat off}
&
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
& 0
\\[3mm]

q_5:\ \text{Load new filament}
&
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & 0
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
& 0
\\[3mm]

q_6:\ \text{Nozzle heat on}
&
\begin{bmatrix}
0 & 0 & u_n
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
& 0
\\[3mm]

q_7:\ \text{Moving back}
&
\begin{bmatrix}
u_x^{\mathrm{return}} &
u_y^{\mathrm{return}} &
u_n
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}
& 0
\end{array}
\]

where \(u_x^{\mathrm{print}}\) and \(u_y^{\mathrm{print}}\) generate the printing trajectory,
\(u_x^{\mathrm{parking}}\) and \(u_y^{\mathrm{parking}}\) move the nozzle towards the wipe-tower position
\((0,0)\), and \(u_x^{\mathrm{return}}\) and \(u_y^{\mathrm{return}}\) return it to the previously stored position.
The input \(u_n\) denotes the nozzle-heater voltage when the heater is active.

For the modes in which the print head remains stationary, we assume that the previous motion has already been completed. Therefore, when entering these modes,

\[
v_x=v_y=i_x=i_y=0,
\]

and

\[
u_x=u_y=0.
\]

Under this assumption, the mechanical system remains at rest and the print-head position does not change.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Memory and logical variables}

To store the position where printing is interrupted, two memory variables \(x_s\) and \(y_s\)
are introduced. During continuous evolution,

\[
\dot{x}_s=0,
\qquad
\dot{y}_s=0.
\]

The extended state is therefore

\[
\xi =
\begin{bmatrix}
x^{\top} & x_s & y_s
\end{bmatrix}^{\top}.
\]

Two binary logical variables are also used,

\[
s,\ell\in\{0,1\},
\]

where \(s=1\) means that the printing position has been saved, while
\(\ell=1\) means that filament is loaded.\\
The starting conditions are

\[
s(0)=0,
\qquad
\ell(0)=1.
\]


\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Invariants}

The invariants associated with the discrete modes are defined as

\[
\begin{array}{r@{\;}c@{\;}l@{\qquad\qquad}r@{\;}c@{\;}l}
\operatorname{Inv}(q_0) &=& \mathcal{X}, &
\operatorname{Inv}(q_4) &=& \{T_n \geq T_{\mathrm{low}}\},\\
\operatorname{Inv}(q_1) &=& \mathcal{X}, &
\operatorname{Inv}(q_5) &=& \{\ell = 0\},\\
\operatorname{Inv}(q_2) &=& \{(x,y)\neq(0,0)\}, &
\operatorname{Inv}(q_6) &=& \{T_n \leq T_{\mathrm{high}}\},\\
\operatorname{Inv}(q_3) &=& \{\ell = 1\}, &
\operatorname{Inv}(q_7) &=& \{(x,y)\neq(x_s,y_s)\}.
\end{array}
\]

In modes \(q_3\) and \(q_5\), the invariant expresses the logical condition that holds until
the corresponding filament status changes. Mode \(q_1\) is considered instantaneous: since \(s=1\) when the
mode is entered, the transition to \(q_2\) can occur immediately.

\medskip
\noindent\textbf{Guards and reset maps}

The transitions between modes are defined by the following guards and reset maps:

\[
\begin{array}{c|c|c}
\text{Transition} & \text{Guard} & \text{Reset map} \\ \hline

q_0\rightarrow q_1
&
C\lor F
&
x_s^+=x^-,
\quad
y_s^+=y^-,
\quad
s^+=1
\\[2mm]

q_1\rightarrow q_2
&
s=1
&
\text{identity}
\\[2mm]

q_2\rightarrow q_3
&
x=0,\quad y=0
&
\text{identity}
\\[2mm]

q_3\rightarrow q_4
&
\ell=0
&
\text{identity}
\\[2mm]

q_4\rightarrow q_5
&
T_n\leq T_{\mathrm{low}}
&
\text{identity}
\\[2mm]

q_5\rightarrow q_6
&
\ell=1
&
\text{identity}
\\[2mm]

q_6\rightarrow q_7
&
T_n\geq T_{\mathrm{high}}
&
\text{identity}
\\[2mm]

q_7\rightarrow q_0
&
x=x_s,\quad y=y_s
&
s^+=0
\end{array}
\]

All variables not changed by a reset keep their previous value.
The resulting hybrid automaton is shown in Figure~\ref{fig:hybrid_filament}.

    \caption{Hybrid automaton describing the filament replacement procedure. The nodes represent the discrete modes, while the edges are labelled by the corresponding guard and reset maps introduced above.}
    \label{fig:hybrid_filament}
\end{figure}
